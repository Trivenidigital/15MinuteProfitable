5. The Stacking Problem

Position Stacking Is The #1 Cause of Losses

fade_panic entered 299 times across only 59 unique markets today — averaging 5.1 entries per market. The worst market had 22 entries.

Why Stacking Is Catastrophic for Binary Markets

  1. Binary payoff: You either win 100% of payout or lose 100% of investment. There's no gradual loss.
  2. When WRONG (75% of the time): ALL entries on that market lose everything. 5 entries = 5x the loss.
  3. When RIGHT (25% of the time): The first entry captures most of the profit. Additional entries at HIGHER prices yield diminishing returns.
  4. Self-adverse selection: The bot's own buying pushes prices UP, making subsequent entries worse.
  5. Asymmetric damage: A single wrong market with 22 entries (-$990) wipes out 10+ winning markets.

6. Signal Anti-Correlation Discovery

fade_panic's Signal Is ANTI-PREDICTIVE

By comparing the direction of each first fade_panic entry against the actual market outcome, we find the signal is systematically wrong:

If the signal were inverted (buy the opposite side), win rate would be 71.2% (42/59 markets).

What This Means

The "panic" fade_panic detects — a rapid odds shift without corresponding spot price movement — is not noise or irrational panic. It's informed trading by market participants who know something. The odds are shifting because the market is correctly pricing in the likely outcome.

By "fading the panic" (buying the cheap side), the bot is systematically betting against informed market participants. This is the exact opposite of what a profitable strategy should do.

Can We Simply Invert the Signal?

No. Due to price symmetry, inverting the signal means buying the expensive side (~$0.70) instead of the cheap side (~$0.25):

  • Inverted win: $0.30 profit per share at 71.2% rate
  • Inverted loss: $0.70 cost per share at 28.8% rate
  • EV = 0.712 × $0.30 - 0.288 × $0.70 = +$0.012/share (1.7% ROI)
  • After fees (3.15% taker + 2% winner): NEGATIVE
Actionable Use of This Finding:
  1. Use as a FILTER: When fade_panic fires, avoid that market entirely. The odds shift is information, not panic.
  2. Combine with external alpha: Only trade when fade_panic signal AGREES with external signals (funding rate, OI delta). Disagreement = market knows something.
  3. Investigate the information source: The rapid odds shifts are coming from somewhere. If we can identify the source (whale wallets? large CLOB orders? cross-exchange arbitrageurs?), we could trade WITH them.

11. Root Cause Analysis

Why the Bot Loses Money (Ranked by Impact)

1. CRITICAL: Uncontrolled Position Stacking

fade_panic enters the same market 5-22 times because max_entries_per_market=50 and cooldown_seconds=0.5. Each wrong market (75% of the time) loses 5-22x what a single entry would lose. The bot also chases its own price impact, buying at $0.25 and continuing to buy up to $0.76.

2. CRITICAL: Signal Is Anti-Predictive (Worse Than Random)

The fade_panic signal doesn't just lack edge — it's actively wrong. When the bot buys NO (betting DOWN), the market goes UP 77% of the time. When it buys YES (betting UP), the market goes DOWN 69% of the time. Overall 27.1% accuracy vs 50% random baseline. The "panic" it fades is actually informed trading. See Section 6 for full analysis.

3. HIGH: Entry Price Indiscriminate

The bot buys at any price up to fade_panic_max_entry_price=0.92. But win rate drops from 35.5% at $0.20-$0.29 to 0% above $0.60. Every entry above $0.40 is statistically a losing bet.

4. MEDIUM: Asymmetric's Negative W/L Ratio

Even with 40% WR, asymmetric loses because avg_win ($43) < avg_loss ($59). The strategy buys both YES and NO sides but combined cost exceeds $1 payout more often than not.

5. MEDIUM: resolution_sniper Buys at Fair Value

With 0.93 W/L ratio and 30% WR, resolution_sniper has no edge. It's buying contracts at approximately their fair probability. Small sizes limit damage.

6. LOW: maker_arbitrage Spreads Too Wide

0% WR across 13 trades. Spreads ($1.01-$1.05) are too wide for profitable arb after fees. Small losses per trade but zero upside.

13. Proposed: Smart Money Signal Strategy

The Core Insight

fade_panic detects sharp odds shifts without corresponding spot price movement. Analysis of 59 markets shows these shifts are informed trading (smart money), not panic — they predict direction correctly 72.9% of the time. Three options were evaluated:

OptionApproachProblem
CurrentFade (bet against) the odds shift27.1% WR — systematically wrong
InvertBuy the expensive side (with the shift)~1.7% ROI pre-fees, negative after taker+winner fees
FilterBlock trading on markets with smart money signalToo conservative — throws away 72.9% accurate directional info
OverrideUse signal to redirect other strategies' directionBest of both worlds — preserves sniper/dip_buyer entry prices + smart money direction

Architecture: Signal-Only fade_panic + Direction Override

fade_panic evaluates market → publishes SmartMoneySignal (direction + metadata)
                                         ↓
              ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
              │  dip_buyer / sniper check signal store   │
              │  Signal exists → override direction      │
              │  No signal → trade natural direction     │
              └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

How It Works

  1. fade_panic becomes signal-only: It keeps all its detection logic (time window, odds shift, spot comparison, oracle cross-validation) but never trades. Instead, it publishes a SmartMoneySignal to a shared store in StateManager.
  2. Two-pass scanner: The MarketScanner evaluates signal-only strategies first (pass 1), then trading strategies second (pass 2). This ensures signals are available before other strategies check for them.
  3. Direction override in sniper/dip_buyer: After determining their natural raw_direction, these strategies check the signal store. If a SmartMoneySignal exists for that market, they override their direction to match smart money. If no signal exists, they trade their natural direction as before.
  4. Sniper reverts to natural direction: The current contrarian inversion (UP → DOWN) is reverted so the override logic is clean: UP → buy YES, DOWN → buy NO.

Direction Logic (Critical)

Odds ShiftMeaningCurrent fade_panicNew Signal Direction
YES odds go UP (>8%) Smart money buying YES Buys NO (fading) — 23.3% WR Signal: UP — sniper/dip_buyer buy YES
YES odds go DOWN (>8%) Smart money buying NO Buys YES (fading) — 31.0% WR Signal: DOWN — sniper/dip_buyer buy NO

Why This Is Better Than the Alternatives

vs. Inverting fade_panic

Inverting means buying the expensive side ($0.70+), yielding only $0.30 profit on win — negative after fees. The override approach keeps sniper/dip_buyer's normal entry prices (typically $0.25-$0.40) while redirecting their direction.

vs. Filtering

Filtering blocks all trading on markets with smart money signals. But those are the markets where we have the best directional information (72.9% accuracy). The override captures this edge instead of discarding it.

Signal Design Decisions

  • Lifetime: Entire 15-minute market window (no decay — binary outcome, direction doesn't change)
  • No strength threshold: Any fade_panic signal overrides direction. The detection thresholds (8% odds shift, spot comparison) already filter noise.
  • No position size changes: Only direction is overridden. Existing profit checks and entry price filters guard against bad entries.
  • Last signal wins: If fade_panic fires multiple times for the same market, the latest direction overwrites (handles changing conditions).

WR_breakeven = 1 / (1 + R)
